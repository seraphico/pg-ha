## 1. Leader Lock ↔ PostgreSQL role

| ID | Statement | Status |
|----|-----------|--------|
| L1 | In any steady state the cluster has at most one **unexpired** `/leader` (leader lock). | holds |
| L2 | On the node that holds the leader lock, PostgreSQL is **not** in recovery (a writable Primary). | holds |
| L3 | On a node that does not hold the lock and serves externally as a replica, PostgreSQL **is** in recovery; or it is in a transitional state defined by the documentation (rewind / rejoin / bootstrap). A transitional state must not claim to be Primary externally. | holds |
| L4 | DCS `members/{name}.role` (and the HA in-memory view) agrees with that node's real PG role; a propagation delay of at most about one HA cycle is allowed, and the upper bound of the delay should be observable. | holds |
| L5 | After a node loses the leader lock, it must not continue to serve externally as a writable Primary (it must stop the database, refuse writes, or enter one of the rejoin paths; the concrete strategy is defined by the implementation, but the result must satisfy this item). **Time bound**: the lock holder becomes unwritable no later than "the moment **before** the last successful lock acquisition / renewal **started** + the TTL written into the lock − `watchdog.safety_margin`", whether or not the DCS call returns (see 1a "leader lease"). Premise: wall-clock skew between nodes is less than `safety_margin` (further minus about 0.1 seconds of fencing time). Exempt under pause (W1). The tail of a graceful shutdown (CHECKPOINT + stopping the database) is likewise constrained by this time bound (1a "leader lease"). | holds |

**Steady-state checks (examples)**

- DCS / `GET /cluster`: the leader is unique
- Lock-holding node: `SELECT pg_is_in_recovery()` → `f`
- Non-lock-holding replica: `pg_is_in_recovery()` → `t`

**Related**: architecture diagrams "HA Decision Loop" and "Election Flow"; operations "Cluster status checks".

### 1a. Identity determination (the basis of L2–L5)

"Whether this node's PG is the primary" is decided only by observation, not by the role recorded in memory (role only records what pg-ha has done before). Implementation: `crates/pg-ha-core/src/ha/identity.rs`.

**Observation**: once PG is confirmed running, each HA cycle runs `pg_is_in_recovery()` once (timeout 5 seconds). After pg-ha itself executes start / stop / promote, the observation is invalidated and must be taken again. A query failure, a timeout, or a process that is not running is recorded as "unobserved".

**Identity** = observation × whether `standby.signal` exists:

| Identity | Observation | `standby.signal` |
|----------|-------------|------------------|
| Primary | not in recovery | not consulted |
| ManagedStandby (a standby configured by pg-ha) | in recovery | present |
| UnmanagedRecovery (recovery not caused by pg-ha) | in recovery | absent |
| Unknown | unobserved | — |

`standby.signal` only says how PG will start next time; it does not say what it is now, so an observation of "not in recovery" is Primary in every case. pg-ha writes `standby.signal` on these occasions: clone, rejoin, switchover demotion, every demotion path (before stopping the database), and every start while not holding the lock (see "Startup rules" below); on the normal election path PG deletes it itself after promote. Therefore "holding the lock and in recovery but without `standby.signal`" is not produced by pg-ha itself; it comes only from `recovery.signal` (archive / PITR) or from a `standby.signal` deleted while running.

**Rule**: pg-ha promotes only a node that it itself configured as a standby. Recovery not caused by pg-ha is handed to the operator: do not promote, do not rewind, and do not take part in the election; if it holds the lock, **stop the database first, then release the lock** (L5: avoid this node becoming writable after `recovery.signal` ends while another node has already acquired the lock).

| Identity | Holds the lock | Another node holds the lock | No leader (election) |
|----------|----------------|-----------------------------|----------------------|
| Primary | stay primary | rejoin (S4); if the leader is not yet running as a primary, stop the database first | take part in the election (must be on the latest timeline, D6; if it cannot take part, stop the database first); if it is not the healthiest, stop it as a stale primary, and next time start as a standby |
| ManagedStandby | promote, and record it as primary only after a fresh observation confirms it | follow; when a rewind-check marker is present, run pg_rewind against the leader first | take part in the election; must be on the latest timeline (D6, with or without the marker) |
| UnmanagedRecovery | stop the database first, then release the lock, and hand it to the operator (when `recovery.signal` is present, do not write `standby.signal`; if it cannot be stopped, keep the lock and fence every cycle with `stop_pending`; under pause, keep the lock, but do not claim to be primary) | hand it to the operator | do not take part in the election |
| Unknown | no action this cycle, and do not claim to be primary | with `standby.signal`: wait; without it: stop the database and fence (L5). After a demotion stop fails, until the stop is confirmed, treat it as fencing in every case (even if `standby.signal` is present) | do not take part in the election this cycle; after a demotion stop fails, stop the database first |

After a demotion stop fails, until PG is confirmed stopped, a node running as a primary also stops the database first at the election entry and does not take part in the election (see "Demotion" below).

**Published externally**: a member's `state` is the same in DCS and in REST. `running` means the process is running and the identity is Primary or ManagedStandby; UnmanagedRecovery and Unknown are published as `unknown`, so other nodes do not treat it as a candidate, a synchronous standby, or a switchover target. `role` is recorded as `primary` only after observation confirms it; when the observation is in recovery, a recorded `primary` is demoted to `replica`.

#### Startup rules

**pg-ha starts PostgreSQL as a primary only while holding the leader lock; in every other case it starts it as a standby.**

- The HA loop starts PG in only one place: `handle_postgres_not_running`. When this node still holds the lock in the view, it calls `delete_leader` first. If the delete returns an error, it does not start (log `Not starting PostgreSQL: the leader lock may still be ours (<错误>). Releasing it again next cycle`) and retries next cycle: the lock may still be ours, and coming up as a standby and then seeing our own lock would promote and undo the switchover. Only after CAS shows the lock is no longer ours, or after the delete succeeds, does it write `standby.signal` and fsync the file and the directory; when the leader is known, is not this node, and its member `conn_url` contains `host=`, it also points `primary_conninfo` at that leader; when the leader is unknown, it keeps the `primary_conninfo` already present in `postgresql.auto.conf`. Log `Starting PostgreSQL as a standby (not holding the leader lock)`.
- If the write fails, it does not start, returns an error, and retries next cycle: a PG that stays stopped loses no data, and a PG that comes up as a writable primary violates L5. Log `Not starting PostgreSQL: could not write the standby configuration (<错误>). pg-ha starts PostgreSQL as a primary only while holding the leader lock; retrying next cycle`.
- Before starting, it reads `Database cluster state` from `pg_controldata` to judge whether the previous run was as a primary, which decides whether to write the rewind-check marker (see below). Failure to write the marker likewise means it does not start.

  | `Database cluster state` | rewind-check marker |
  |--------------------------|---------------------|
  | `in production`, `shut down`, `shutting down`, `in crash recovery`, `starting up` | write (`Created rewind-check marker: pg_controldata reports '<state>' (last ran as a primary)`) |
  | an unrecognized value, or a failure to read `pg_controldata` | write (on a read failure, WARN `Could not read the cluster state from pg_controldata (...); creating the rewind-check marker to be safe`) |
  | `shut down in recovery`, `in archive recovery` | do not write |

  `shutting down` is a primary that crashed in the middle of shutting down; `in crash recovery` appears only on a start that does not have `standby.signal`. An uncertain state is handled in the conservative direction: writing the marker one extra time only adds one pg_rewind check (when there is no divergence it immediately reports `no rewind required`). A node whose first start after clone failed has, in `pg_controldata`, the `in production` copied from the source, so the next start also carries the marker; the cost is likewise only one check.
- Other start entry points — start after clone, start after pg_rewind, and restart after the upstream is reconfigured — all write the standby configuration first, and do not start if the write fails. `POST /restart` is unchanged while the lock is held; while the lock is not held it first does the same preparation as above (including the `pg_controldata` marker), and if the write fails it refuses and PG is not touched; it refuses while in the rewind-failure hold. The only exception is the node running initdb during bootstrap: it won the atomic race for `/initialize`, it is the only node in the cluster that has data, a database produced by initdb has no upstream it can follow, and it acquires the lock immediately after starting.
- After a graceful shutdown has begun, pg-ha no longer starts PostgreSQL (`Postgresql::block_starts`; every start entry goes through `Postgresql::start`). When the HA loop finds that PG has stopped, it only ends this cycle (`PostgreSQL stopped; graceful shutdown finishing`), and does not release the lock or start PG; the shutdown flow releases the lock after the stop is confirmed.
- Cost: after a primary crash or a brief DCS interruption, the node restarts as a standby and can become a primary again only through an election + promote (timeline +1); a cold start of the whole cluster also goes through an election.
- When a node is stopped and `recovery.signal` is present, starting it makes pg-ha write `standby.signal` as well; standby mode takes precedence, and the recovery target of archive / PITR does not run as expected. Stop pg-ha before doing archive recovery or PITR.

#### Demotion: write `standby.signal` first, then stop the database

- `demote()` (lock renewal failed), both demotion branches of `handle_dcs_error`, Fence, and switchover write `standby.signal` **before stopping the database**, and fsync the file and the directory; the first two are running as a primary and also write the rewind-check marker. If the write fails, the database is still stopped (L5 takes priority), an ERROR is logged, and an error is returned; afterwards the startup rules refuse to start when `standby.signal` is absent.
- This rule applies to every path that stops a PG that "may be a writable primary not holding the lock", not only the ones above: stopping, before rejoin, a PG that is running as a primary; stopping when the rewind source is not ready; stopping a stale primary; stopping a primary that has the marker and cannot take part in the election; a primary started by hand during a rewind-failure hold; `POST /reinitialize` stopping a PG that is running as a primary; `POST /restart` (when not holding the lock); graceful shutdown (first stop a PG that is running as a primary or cannot be observed: `-m fast`, and on failure try `-m immediate` once more; release the lock only after the stop succeeds; if it cannot be stopped, do not exit); and fencing again after a demotion stop fails (including the election entry). All of them write `standby.signal` first (and also write the marker if it has run as a primary) and then stop the database. When `POST /reinitialize` meets such a PG and the stop fails, it does not delete the data directory. A read-only standby (observed in recovery and with `standby.signal`) may be stopped directly.
- When a stop fails, until PG is confirmed stopped (`pg_ctl stop` succeeded, or some cycle observes that PG is no longer running), an unobservable PG is treated as fencing in every case: the stop is retried every cycle, even if `standby.signal` is already present. The reason is that this signal was written on a PG that may be writable, so it can no longer be taken as proof that the PG is not writable.
- After a demotion stop succeeds, the node immediately publishes member state once (`state=stopped`), so that other nodes see that it has stopped as soon as possible. Publication is time-bounded (no more than `loop_wait`); when DCS is unreachable it does not stall the HA loop or shutdown.
- **Lease-expiry fencing** (a demotion entry alongside `demote()`, see the next subsection): when the leader lease expires, it no longer waits for any DCS call to return, discards this HA cycle, first writes `standby.signal` and the rewind-check marker (reason `leader lease expired before renewal`), then runs `pg_ctl stop -m immediate`, and records this node as not holding the lock. If the stop fails, it keeps fencing every cycle under the `stop_pending` rule above.
- **DCS writes have an upper bound**: every DCS write (`propose` of `RaftDcs`, including both the local Raft commit path and the path that forwards to the Raft leader) waits at most `retry_timeout` seconds and, on timeout, returns `Error::Timeout`. The caller treats it as a failed write (a lock-renewal timeout is a demotion). Log WARN `DCS write timed out after <n>s (retry_timeout); treating it as failed`. Reason: openraft 0.9 has no check-quorum, a Raft leader that has lost the majority does not step down, and its `client_write` returns only when the network recovers.
- **A late commit after a timeout is safe**: a write that was given up may still commit later. (1) This node is the Raft leader that lost the majority: after the majority elects a new leader, this uncommitted log entry is truncated on recovery and is not committed; it can commit in the original term only when the partition is shorter than the majority's re-election time. (2) This node is a Raft follower: the request has already been handed to the leader and may commit after HA has given up. Lock renewal is a CAS (`prev_value` = this node's name): it fails when a new primary has already written the lock; when the lock has expired by real time but nobody has acquired it, the state machine judges by the old `now` in the request and renews the lock until this request's time + TTL. This does not produce two lock holders: acquiring the lock is also a CAS, the two are ordered in the Raft log, and the later one fails; promote happens only on the path that holds the lock. A lock that got renewed only makes the majority wait a while longer; this node has already demoted (lock renewal failed, or the lease expired). Next cycle, while PostgreSQL is still stopped and the lock is still ours in the view, delete the lock first: if the delete fails, do not start, and try again next cycle. It will not come up as a standby and then be promoted while the lock may still be ours. Only after the delete succeeds, or CAS shows the lock is no longer ours, does it start as a standby. Nobody else promoted while this node still held the lock, so the data has not diverged. A late commit only makes the lock live longer in DCS, and the lease is anchored on the last **successful** request, so it does not let the old primary write any more.

#### Leader lease (the time bound of L5)

- Anchor: **before** every call to `attempt_to_acquire_leader` / `update_leader`, record both the monotonic clock (`Instant`) and the wall clock (`SystemTime`) at the same time. After the call returns `Ok(true)`, the lease deadline = anchor + `dcs.ttl()` (the TTL actually written into the lock, not the `ttl` in the dynamic configuration) − `watchdog.safety_margin`, computed once on the monotonic clock and once on the wall clock. `Ok(false)` or `Err` does not extend the lease. The anchor is taken before the call, and the `now` that RaftDcs takes when it constructs the request can only be later, so the lock's expiry time in DCS is not earlier than anchor + TTL, and the lease is conservative.
- Decision: the lease expires when either the monotonic clock or the wall clock reaches the deadline. The wall-clock decision covers this node's wall clock being stepped backward, and the monotonic clock not advancing while the system is suspended (DCS expiry is judged by the wall clock).
- Checkpoints: the lease is checked both before `pg_ctl promote` and before reporting Hold (staying primary). On expiry, treat it as losing the lock: run lease-expiry fencing, and log ERROR `Leader lease expired before pg_ctl promote; fencing PostgreSQL (immediate stop)` or `Leader lease expired before reporting the leader role; fencing PostgreSQL (immediate stop)`.
- Bounded cycle: each HA cycle races the lease deadline (`Ha::run_cycle_bounded`; `src/main.rs` calls only that). When the deadline arrives first, discard this cycle (a discarded DCS write may commit late; see above) and run the lease-expiry fencing of the previous subsection. Log ERROR `Leader lease expired before the lock renewal completed; fencing PostgreSQL (immediate stop) without waiting for the DCS`. Between cycles the HA loop waits only for the timer (bounded by the parameter constraint below) and the write lock of the REST API state (API handlers hold it only while reading locally), and does no other unbounded await: refreshing proxy backends does a DNS resolution that has no timeout, so it is placed in a separate task, and each HA cycle only hands it the member list (`refresh_proxy_backends` in `src/main.rs`; the test `test_ha_loop_awaits_nothing_unbounded_outside_the_bounded_cycle` checks this). The keep-running loop entered when a graceful shutdown cannot stop PG (`keep_running_until_stopped`) likewise waits only for the timer, the bounded cycle, and the API state write lock, and each cycle also calls `Ha::continue_shutdown`, which waits for one DCS write (deleting the lock) only after PG is confirmed stopped.
- Time bound: the old primary's last writable moment ≤ anchor + TTL − `safety_margin` + ε (ε is the time to wake the timer, write the signal and the marker and fsync them, and send SIGQUIT, about 0.1 seconds); the majority acquires the lock at the earliest at anchor + TTL − δ (δ is how far the majority's wall clock is ahead of the old primary), and after that still needs a Raft commit, writing the pending of `/timeline`, and promote. So as long as δ < `safety_margin` − ε there is no overlap, independent of whether the DCS call returns.
- Parameter constraint: `ttl ≥ loop_wait + retry_timeout + watchdog.safety_margin`, and `retry_timeout ≥ 1`. A healthy primary must finish one HA cycle plus one bounded lock-renewal write inside the lease. When the static configuration (including the values after environment-variable overrides, and `bootstrap.dcs`) does not satisfy it, startup is refused; when a `PATCH` / `PUT /config` changes `ttl`, `loop_wait`, or `retry_timeout` and the result does not satisfy it, return 400 and do not write the configuration. An item omitted from the dynamic configuration is computed from the static value of the node that received the request.
- Cost: when PG or DCS is so slow that one lock renewal cannot finish inside the lease (for example, under the docker configuration `pg_is_in_recovery()` approaches its 5-second timeout), a healthy primary is fenced too, and the cluster has one extra failover. This trades availability for L5.
- Exempt under pause: consistent with staying primary when lock renewal fails under pause (W1; under pause other nodes do not acquire the lock automatically), lease expiry does not fence.
- Graceful shutdown (`Ha::graceful_shutdown`; `src/main.rs` calls only that):
  1. On the first SIGTERM / SIGINT, mark shutdown: `/primary` is 503 from then on (H1), pg-ha no longer starts PG (startup rules), and management commands are rejected in every case (`Graceful shutdown in progress; command rejected`). The REST API keeps serving throughout the shutdown.
  2. Check the lease (before drain): if this node is recorded as holding the lock and the lease has already expired, skip drain, CHECKPOINT, and releasing the lock, and run lease-expiry fencing directly. Log ERROR `Leader lease already expired at shutdown; skipping drain, CHECKPOINT and lock release, fencing PostgreSQL (immediate stop)`.
  3. During drain (`shutdown.drain_timeout_secs`), renew the lock once every `loop_wait`, and the last renewal is at the end of drain. If renewal fails, fence immediately (log ERROR `Leader lock renewal failed during shutdown drain; fencing PostgreSQL (immediate stop) and not releasing the lock`), do not CHECKPOINT, and do not release the lock. Exception under pause (W1): a failed renewal only logs WARN `Leader lock renewal failed during shutdown drain in pause mode; not fencing (pause mode), continuing the shutdown`, drain continues, and the next renewal is a retry.
  4. After drain, the lease of the last successful renewal (anchor + `dcs.ttl()` − `safety_margin`) bounds CHECKPOINT and the stop, racing the same way as `run_cycle_bounded`; log INFO `Shutdown drain finished; leader lock renewed. CHECKPOINT and the PostgreSQL stop must finish within <n>s (leader lease), otherwise PostgreSQL is fenced`. The stop tries `-m fast` first, and if that fails while PG is still running, tries `-m immediate` once more (WARN `pg_ctl stop -m fast failed during shutdown (...); retrying once with -m immediate`). When the deadline arrives first, discard the remaining steps and run lease-expiry fencing (write `standby.signal` and the rewind-check marker first, then `pg_ctl stop -m immediate`), and do not release the lock. Log ERROR `Leader lease expired before CHECKPOINT and the PostgreSQL stop finished during shutdown; fencing PostgreSQL (immediate stop) and keeping the leader lock`. Under pause, and when not holding the lock, there is no race.
  5. Call `delete_leader` only after PG is confirmed stopped (DCS writes have an upper bound, and wait at most `retry_timeout`). Log `Leader lock released after PostgreSQL stopped`; if the delete fails or is rejected, the lock is left in place and expires by its TTL (`Leader lock kept: the delete failed or was rejected; it expires after its TTL`).
  6. When PG may be writable but cannot be stopped (both the fast stop and the immediate stop failed, or the fencing immediate stop failed, that is `stop_pending`), the process **does not exit**: it keeps running the normal HA loop (while holding the lock, renew every cycle and retry the stop every cycle under `stop_pending`, and do not promote; while not holding the lock, each cycle only stops the database once more with `-m immediate`, and does not take part in the election, follow, or promote; WARN `Graceful shutdown: PostgreSQL is still running without the leader lock; stopping it again (immediate)`), does not start PG, rejects management commands, and every cycle logs ERROR `Graceful shutdown could not stop PostgreSQL; pg-ha keeps running instead of exiting while PostgreSQL may be writable (...). Stop PostgreSQL manually as the PostgreSQL OS user: <bin_dir>/pg_ctl stop -D <data_dir> -m immediate; pg-ha then releases the lock and exits`. A later SIGTERM / SIGINT only logs WARN `Received <sig> during graceful shutdown; pg-ha exits only after PostgreSQL has stopped` and makes the next cycle run immediately (retry the stop immediately); it does not make the process exit. After PG has stopped, a node that holds the lock releases the lock and then exits; a node that was fenced and no longer holds the lock exits directly. "May be writable" includes two cases observed in recovery: this node holds the lock (promote failed, promote is in progress, or a signal interrupted the cycle between acquiring the lock and promote, and pg-ha will promote it), and recovery without `standby.signal` (such as `recovery.signal`, which promotes itself when recovery ends). When either of these cannot be stopped, also set `stop_pending` and do not exit; the latter does not write `standby.signal` (do not alter the operator's recovery). Only when a standby that does not hold the lock, is observed in recovery, and has `standby.signal` cannot be stopped does it log ERROR `PostgreSQL (a standby with standby.signal, not the leader lock holder) could not be stopped; exiting, nothing promotes it` and then exit (`identity::shutdown_may_leave_running`).
  - Switchover likewise stops the database first and then releases the lock; when the stop fails the lock stays, and the next cycle continues fencing while holding the lock.
- Not covered: when the HA loop is blocked by a synchronous call, the tokio workers are saturated, or the process is stopped by SIGSTOP or the VM is frozen, the race cannot run, and only the watchdog can act as the backstop (W2; the default is `mode: off`, and `automatic` or `required` must be configured). When the process is killed by SIGKILL (systemd `TimeoutStopSec` expires, or `docker stop -t` expires), the shutdown rules cannot run: with systemd `KillMode=mixed`, the final SIGKILL kills every process in the unit cgroup, including the PG that pg-ha started, so PG crashes and is not left writable; when PG is not in pg-ha's cgroup it stays writable and nobody fences it. In docker the container's PID 1 is pg-ha, and when it exits PG is killed with it.
- When the leader is unknown at demotion (`demote`, `handle_dcs_error`, a switchover with no candidate, graceful shutdown), delete `primary_conninfo` from `postgresql.auto.conf` and do not keep a guess. The upstream is configured only by the follow path, from the leader that was observed, including the case where `postgresql.auto.conf` has no `primary_conninfo`.
- When writing the standby configuration, `postgresql.auto.conf` replaces only `primary_conninfo` and `recovery_target_timeline`; every other line (content written by `ALTER SYSTEM`, such as `synchronous_standby_names`) is kept; the file is written through a temporary file + rename and is fsynced. When a member `conn_url` has no `host=`, treat the leader as unknown, and do not write a `primary_conninfo` that points at `127.0.0.1`. Every time a node leaves the primary role or rewrites the standby configuration, pg-ha forgets the `synchronous_standby_names` it applied last time, so the first time of the next primary term always runs `ALTER SYSTEM` again and writes `/sync` (C4).

#### Rewind-check marker (`PGDATA/pg-ha.rewind_check`)

- Meaning: this node may have WAL after the divergence point, and it must be verified with pg_rewind before following. The marker decides only whether to do the rewind check; it does not decide identity, and it does not change the health decision.
- Who writes it: the demotion paths (the previous section), and the startup rules (when `pg_controldata` shows that the previous run was as a primary). Who deletes it: after pg_rewind succeeds, including `no rewind required` (log `pg_rewind succeeded; removed the rewind-check marker`); `pg-ha.rewind_check` and `pg-ha.rewind_failed` copied from the source by clone and by pg_rewind are also deleted, because they describe the source; `POST /reinitialize` deletes them together with the data directory.
- A ManagedStandby that has the marker, when another node holds the lock and the leader is ready, takes the rejoin path: stop the local PG, run pg_rewind against the leader, then start as a standby and follow (log `Rewind-check marker present and <leader> holds the leader lock — checking with pg_rewind before following`). When the leader is not ready, keep running as a read-only standby and wait (`rewind-check marker present; waiting for <leader> to run as a primary before pg_rewind`).
- "Leader ready": in the DCS view it holds the lock, and the member record is `state=running` and `role=primary`; **and** a live connection that uses its `conn_url` runs `SELECT pg_is_in_recovery()` and gets `f` (5-second connect timeout, 10-second total timeout; failure to connect, a timeout, or an answer of `t` all count as not ready). The live confirmation is done twice: once before stopping the local PG, and once again after the stop and before running pg_rewind. That way, pg_rewind is not run against the source during the few seconds after the source has just stopped while the member record in DCS has not yet been updated, nor when the source is demoted during the local stop (a large database has to be flushed to disk, which can be slow).
- Every pg_rewind uses only a ready lock holder as the source. When the source is not ready, do not run pg_rewind, do not count it as a failure, and do not enter the "rewind failed" hold: log INFO `Not running pg_rewind: <leader> <原因>`; if the local PG's identity is primary, stop the database first (write `standby.signal` and the marker first); when the second confirmation fails, PG is already stopped, so stay stopped, and next cycle start as a standby and then wait.
- A stale primary discovered when there is no lock holder (running as a primary, and not the healthiest node) is only stopped, and is not rewound; next time it starts as a standby (with the marker), and it rewinds after a leader appears. If it is the only node that can be elected, it takes part in the election under the rules for a node that has the marker.
- A node publishes `rewind_check` in its member data (whether the marker file exists), for other nodes to use during the election and during switchover (see the next subsection). An old-version node does not publish this field and is treated as not having the marker; during a rolling upgrade an old node may still yield to a new-version node that has the marker and will not take part in the election.
- A standby that has the marker, while it is waiting for the rewind check (the leader is not ready, during a partition, and after the network recovers but before pg_rewind), still runs as a read-only standby. Its `/patroni` state is `running`, but **`/replica` returns 503** (H2). Proxy RO does not treat it as a healthy replica, so that a diverged history is not read.

#### D6: timeline identity, election eligibility, and history generation (`/timeline`)

Invariants:

- **I1 numbers are unique**: each timeline number managed by pg-ha corresponds to only one history.
- **I2 record the true value**: `/timeline` records the identity of the latest confirmed timeline (number + begin LSN, that is, the switch point on the last line of that number's history file; the begin of timeline 1 is 0), and a promote that may be in progress (`pending`). At any moment, a timeline that exists in the cluster is either not higher than the recorded value, or is the one produced by that pending promote.
- **I3 election eligibility**: only a node that is on the latest timeline may take part in the election, with or without the rewind-check marker.

I1 follows from I2 and I3: the number PostgreSQL assigns to a new timeline is `findNewestTimeLine(recoveryTargetTLI) + 1`. It probes `pg_wal/<TLI>.history` one by one, starting from the recovery target (and it also probes the archive when `restore_command` is configured). On a node that is on the latest timeline L, the local newest history is L, so the new number is L + 1; when there is no pending, no node has L + 1. The consequence of a repeated number: pg_rewind compares two histories by "number + begin", and when two different histories have both the same number and the same begin, pg_rewind treats them as the same history (measured on docker on 2026-10-27: two TL5s both began at 0/402B5F0); when archiving is on, files of the same name conflict.

- Key: `/<namespace>/<scope>/timeline`, **no TTL**, JSON, for example `{"timeline":5,"begin":67286512,"primary":"node1","generation":0,"pending":{...}}`. Every write is a CAS against the version that was read (when the key does not exist, only creation is allowed); it does not merge, and it does not lower the value.
- The old format (one decimal number, for example `"17"`) is used only as an upper bound, and is never used as an identity; a key that does not exist or cannot be parsed is handled the same way (see item 3 of the decision below).
- The `timeline` in a member record is the timeline of the `wal_position` that member publishes (R2, the same WAL point). A primary takes `pg_walfile_name(pg_current_wal_lsn())`, which is an exact value. A replica, while its WAL receiver is streaming and its `flushed_lsn` is not below the replay position, takes `pg_stat_wal_receiver.received_tli` (read from the same row as `flushed_lsn`), which is an exact value; otherwise it takes a lower bound, the maximum, among the following, that is known not to be above the published position: the timeline from `pg_control_checkpoint()`, the current receiver's pair of values, and the pair of values the receiver last reported during this PostgreSQL run (a change of `pg_postmaster_start_time()` invalidates it, because both pg_rewind and pg_basebackup stop the database). A lower bound can only be too low, and cannot be too high.
- What a member publishes (`lineage`; old-version nodes do not have it): `timeline_begin` (the begin of the published `timeline`, taken from the local history file); `history` (the history with the largest number in `pg_wal`: the number, the begin, and whether a WAL segment named with that number exists locally, `wal`); `postmaster_start` (lines 1 and 3 of `postmaster.pid`: the PID and the start time; empty when PostgreSQL is stopped); `generation` (`PGDATA/pg-ha.generation`, and 0 when the file does not exist). If any one item cannot be read, the whole `lineage` is not published (WARN `Not publishing timeline evidence: ...`), and nothing is guessed.

**Determining the latest timeline L** (`ha::lineage::resolve_latest`, a pure function; every node reaches the same conclusion from the same inputs):

1. There is no pending in the record: L is the record.
2. There is a pending (initiator R, base T, generation g):
   - A member of generation g publishes a `history` whose number is higher than T: R has already produced a new timeline (PostgreSQL writes the history file before it switches, and the history file existing is enough), and L takes this identity. When several members give different identities, it is judged a conflict, and no node takes part in the election (ERROR).
   - The `history` published by R is not higher than T, and R's PostgreSQL is stopped (`stopped`/`crashed`), or `postmaster_start` differs from the one recorded in the pending: that promote can no longer finish (the postmaster deletes a leftover promote signal file when it starts; a backend crash and restart does not change `postmaster.pid`), the pending is voided, and L is the recorded value.
   - Otherwise it is unresolved: **only R may take part in the election** (it may hold the newest data; after it is elected again it promotes again).
3. There is no record in the current format (old format, absent, or unparseable): only when every Raft voter has published `history` (the number of members is not less than the number of voters, and every member has published) and the members' generations are the same does L take the history with the largest number among all members. The begin is given by the member that holds it, and the begins must agree. Otherwise it is unresolved (incomplete, the generations disagree, or there is a conflict), and no node takes part in the election.
4. When a primary holds the lock, during Hold it writes the record as its own identity (it is the current writer); the old format is migrated by this.

**Election eligibility** (`ha::lineage::eligibility`; when a node judges itself it uses the record it published, A9): when L is known, the member must be the same generation as L and must satisfy any one of the following, with a begin that agrees with L:

- The published `timeline` equals L. A lower bound that equals L also counts: the true value is not below L, and I2 guarantees that no timeline higher than L exists.
- `history` equals L and `wal` is true. This covers "a primary that was fenced after promote and before the first checkpoint".

The same number with a different begin, or a local history higher than L: ERROR, and do not take part. No published `lineage`: do not take part. When L is unresolved, only the pending initiator may take part. When a node cannot take part, log INFO `Not taking part in the election: <原因>` (a conflict is ERROR, and the same line is logged only once); a node that cannot take part and is running as a primary stops the database first (L5; write `standby.signal` and the marker first).

- Every eligible node is on L, and "who is healthiest" compares only LSN, then `failover_priority`, then the name (the "compare timeline first" added on 2026-10-26 is no longer needed). The rewind-check marker no longer affects the election: a node on L cannot have diverged, because L has been written only by its own primary. The marker is still used for "rewind first, then follow" and for the candidate check of switchover/failover.
- When the designated candidate cannot take part, the other nodes continue as if there were no candidate (A25), and still exclude `/failover.leader` (S1).

**promote** (the lock-holding node R):

1. Self-check: the local newest history must be L itself (or L comes from R's own unresolved pending); otherwise refuse to promote (ERROR `Not promoting: this node's newest history is ..., not the latest ...; promoting would reuse a timeline number`).
2. CAS-write the pending (R, the base, and the generation; `postmaster_start` is taken from the current `postmaster.pid`). If the write fails or the version has already changed, do not promote. A retry within the same term reuses this pending and does not rewrite it.
3. `pg_ctl promote`. After observing that it is not in recovery, read the identity from `pg_walfile_name(pg_current_wal_lsn())` and from the new history file, read the key again, confirm that the pending is still this node's, and then CAS-write the confirmed record (clearing the pending). On failure the pending is kept (it continues to block the other nodes), and Hold retries.
4. Hold (holding the lock and being the primary), every cycle: if the record agrees with its own identity, do nothing; if the record is missing, is in the old format, is lower than itself, or is its own pending, write it; if the record is higher than itself, has the same number and a different begin, is another node's pending that is still unresolved, or the generation disagrees, do not write, and log ERROR. The standby leader of a standby cluster does not promote; during Hold it writes, as the identity, the local newest history that has WAL.

**History generation and operator-forced election**:

- The cluster does not elect a primary automatically in the following cases: every node on L is permanently lost; the pending initiator is permanently lost; the decision is a conflict. This is the cost of I3, because electing a primary automatically would drop the newer history and repeat a number.
- After the operator confirms that the loss is acceptable, place `pg-ha.accept_timeline_loss` in the chosen node's PGDATA. It takes effect only when no other node can take part in the election; otherwise WARN and ignore it. That node takes part and promotes, and the pending's generation is the recorded generation + 1; after the promote is confirmed, first persist the local `pg-ha.generation`, then write the record of the new generation, and then delete that file.
- A forced election **necessarily repeats a number**: a lagging node's local history is below a number that already exists; putting a newer history file into `pg_wal` would change the recovery target and produce an illegal history, which is not feasible. So only the generation can distinguish them. Data whose generation is below the cluster generation does not take part, does not start, does not follow, and does not rewind; `POST /restart` is rejected and PostgreSQL is stopped (the ERROR gives `POST /reinitialize`). After a fresh clone, the new data directory carries the source's `pg-ha.generation` along with pg_basebackup (A16 does not delete it). While a forced election's pending is unresolved, nodes of the old generation likewise stay stopped, until it is confirmed or voided.

#### pg_rewind failure

- pg_rewind must be executed, and it is executed automatically.
- When pg_rewind fails, or when pg_rewind succeeds but the following PG start fails, the default (`postgresql.remove_data_directory_on_rewind_failure: false`) is: PG stays stopped; the data directory is not deleted and is not rebuilt; write `PGDATA/pg-ha.rewind_failed`; do not take part in the election; do not retry automatically. The rewind-check marker: it is kept when pg_rewind fails; when PG fails to start after pg_rewind succeeds, the marker has already been deleted after the rewind succeeded, and only `pg-ha.rewind_failed` remains. On entering the hold, pg-ha first confirms that PG has stopped, then logs one ERROR, and afterwards repeats it at most once every 60 seconds:

  ```text
  <pg_rewind | Starting PostgreSQL after pg_rewind> failed; PostgreSQL is stopped and its data directory is kept (pg-ha did not delete or rebuild it). Cause: <原因>. Data directory: <dir> (<大小>). pg-ha will not start, rewind or rebuild this node on its own. Operator action required: call POST /reinitialize on this node to delete the local data and copy it again from the primary with pg_basebackup (a full copy, about <大小>); or set postgresql.remove_data_directory_on_rewind_failure: true and restart pg-ha to let it rebuild automatically. If the cause was temporary, delete <dir>/pg-ha.rewind_failed to let pg-ha retry pg_rewind once (no pg-ha restart needed).
  ```

  `<原因>` is taken from the error output of pg_rewind (or of `pg_ctl start`). Only `error:` / `fatal:` lines are kept (when there are none, all of it is kept), and multiple lines are joined with ` | `. `<大小>` is the sum of the sizes of the files under the data directory, counted with a limit of 2 seconds; on timeout it is `size unknown`, and when a subdirectory cannot be read it is `at least <大小>`.

  The log writes "data directory is kept" rather than "unchanged": when pg_rewind fails during the copy phase it may already have changed the directory, and pg-ha can guarantee only that it itself did not delete or rebuild it. This is also why it does not start automatically during the hold.
- Handling: `POST /reinitialize`; or turn that option on and restart pg-ha; when the cause is temporary and pg_rewind failed before it changed the directory (for example a connection error), delete `pg-ha.rewind_failed`. No pg-ha restart is needed (log `Rewind hold file <dir>/pg-ha.rewind_failed was removed; pg-ha will retry pg_rewind`), and the next cycle starts that node as a standby. The case where pg_rewind failed (the marker is still present): after the leader is seen to be ready, run pg_rewind once more, and if it fails again enter the hold again. The case where the start failed after pg_rewind succeeded (the marker has already been deleted): first retry the start as a standby, and run pg_rewind against the leader once more only after 3 consecutive start failures.
- A PG that is started by hand during the hold is also stopped by pg-ha. The hold state is recorded in `pg-ha.rewind_failed` and stays valid across a pg-ha restart; when writing this file fails, the hold exists only in memory and lasts until pg-ha restarts (ERROR `Could not write <dir>/pg-ha.rewind_failed (...); the hold lasts until pg-ha restarts`).
- When the option is turned on, the old behavior returns: delete the data directory and then run pg_basebackup (WARN `pg_rewind failed: <错误> — remove_data_directory_on_rewind_failure is enabled, rebuilding with pg_basebackup`).
- Cases that do not count as a failure: the source is not ready (the previous subsection); after pg_rewind succeeds, the wait of `pg_ctl start -w` times out, but the postmaster is still running (a standby does not accept connections before it has replayed to a consistent point, which is common on a large database). That is only a WARN `PostgreSQL is still starting after pg_rewind (...); not treating this as a failure`, and the next cycle continues by observation. "PG failed to start" means only that the postmaster did not come up.

## 2. Health checks ↔ identity

Health checks are what is told externally; they must reach the same ending as the L\* story.

| ID | Statement | Status |
|----|-----------|--------|
| H1 | `GET /primary` returns 200 ⟺ this node holds the leader lock, and PG is a healthy Primary, and it is not in graceful shutdown. | holds |
| H2 | `GET /replica` returns 200 ⟺ this node is a healthy Replica (when the optional `lag` threshold is satisfied; when lag is unknown, treat it as passing, fail-open), and there is **no** `pg-ha.rewind_check` marker. | holds |
| H3 | At the same moment, at most one node in the cluster returns 200 for `/primary`. | holds |
| H4 | TCP Proxy: the health probe of the RW port depends on `/primary`; RO depends on `/replica` (and on the fallback the documentation specifies). | holds |
| H5 | When `synchronous_mode` is false, or DCS `/sync` is empty (equivalent to the synchronous list not being enabled): every healthy Replica is 200 for `/async` and 503 for `/sync`; the Primary is 503 for both. | holds |
| H6 | When `synchronous_mode` is true and `/sync` has been published: the set of nodes for which `/sync` returns 200 = the intersection of the parsed result of `/sync.sync_standby` and the healthy Replicas; every other healthy Replica is 200 for `/async`. | holds |
| H7 | After Switchover / Failover completes, H1–H4 hold again within the agreed time limit (suggested: ≤ `2 * loop_wait` + the time for promote/reconfiguration); if synchronous replication is enabled, H6 holds at the same time. | holds |

**Per-clause decision for H1 / H2** (`crates/pg-ha-api/src/state.rs`; the data lags by at most one HA cycle):

- `/primary` 200 ⟺ holding the leader lock ∧ `state = running` ∧ `role = primary` ∧ the observation is not in recovery ∧ not in graceful shutdown. When the observation is missing, it is 503.
- Graceful shutdown: from the receipt of SIGTERM / SIGINT, `/primary` is 503 in every case (`shutting_down`), and the Proxies of the other nodes no longer route new writes to this node (the same routing effect as the old shutdown, in which REST had already stopped); the REST API keeps serving throughout the shutdown, and `/patroni` and `/cluster` report the role and the lock as they actually are. `/liveness` becomes 503 during drain (there is no HA cycle).
- `/replica` 200 ⟺ `state = running` ∧ `role = replica` ∧ the observation is in recovery ∧ there is no rewind-check marker (and then the `lag` threshold is applied on top: return 503 only when lag is **known** and greater than the threshold; when lag is unknown, still return 200, with `"lag": null` in the response. Reason: during failover there is no leader, every replica's lag is unknown, and fail-closed would take every replica out of the read pool at that moment). A node observed as not in recovery does not serve externally as a replica, whatever role is recorded. A node that has `pg-ha.rewind_check` returns 503, so that an RO client does not read data that sits on a diverged history.
- `/health` reflects only whether the PG process is running, and is unrelated to the HA identity.
- `replication_lag` = the `wal_position` last published in the leader's member record, minus this node's own `wal_position` (bytes, minimum 0; `ha::lag::replication_lag_bytes`, a pure function). This node's position is the same value that `touch_member` publishes to DCS (a primary uses `pg_current_wal_lsn`, a replica uses `max(receive, replay)`, R2). It reuses the same `wal_status` and does not add another PostgreSQL query. Notes: (1) the leader's position is refreshed only once per `loop_wait`, so when writes are very heavy the lag is too small, short by at most about one `loop_wait` of WAL; (2) it measures WAL the replica **has received** (the greater of receive and replay), not WAL that has been applied, so when it is used to judge read freshness it may be too optimistic; (3) the primary reports 0; when a replica has no leader record, or the position on either side is missing (including PostgreSQL not running), it is **unknown**: the JSON is `null`, `/metrics` omits the `pg_ha_replication_lag_bytes` line (HELP/TYPE are kept), and it is not treated as 0. The snapshot published by the demotion / fencing path (`publish_not_primary`) sets `replication_lag` to unknown (the lag of a primary that has just been demoted, relative to the new leader, is not 0), while `timeline` / `wal_position` keep the last sample.
- Demotion / fencing does not wait for this cycle to finish: `stop_after_demotion_write` publishes API state once before the stop and once after the stop (before the post-stop `touch_member`) (not holding the lock, role is not primary, observation missing), so `/primary` becomes 503 no later than PG becomes unwritable. The DCS write after the stop may wait out the full `retry_timeout` on a minority, which does not affect this (`Ha::set_state_publisher`, test `test_demotion_publishes_non_primary_before_stop_and_touch_member`).

**Steady-state check**: the curl examples for Primary / Replica / sync / async in the operations guide; a three-node cross probe of H3; the `pg_in_recovery` field of `GET /patroni` is this node's most recent observation.

## 3. Switch stories (Switchover / Failover)

| ID | Statement | Status |
|----|-----------|--------|
| S1 | **Switchover**: provided the candidate can take over, the old primary demotes and releases the lock → the new primary acquires the lock and promotes → replica upstreams point at the new primary; after completion L\* and H\* hold. When no candidate is specified, the new primary is the healthiest eligible node other than `/failover.leader`; that leader does not take part in the election, and the other nodes do not count it in "who is healthiest". | holds |
| S2 | **Failover**: after the lock expires or the primary is confirmed unavailable, only eligible candidates take part in the election; the winner promotes and writes the lock; after completion L\* and H\* hold. | holds |
| S3 | After a switch completes, `GET /cluster`, the members' role/state, and the real topology agree; `GET /history` (DCS `/history`) records this failover / switchover **best-effort**: when the new primary acquires the lock it records, in memory only, one entry waiting to be written (event type, old primary, reason), and the first Hold cycle afterwards CAS-appends it to `/history` (a bounded timeout; on a version conflict it retries at most 3 times, and on failure it retries next cycle). The promote path does not write DCS. A failed history write does not change the cycle's result, does not demote, and does not fence. Therefore a crash of the new primary between acquiring the lock and the append loses this one entry; the cluster's initial bootstrap is not recorded; the standby leader of a standby cluster does not record one. History is not an audit log. | holds (best-effort) |
| S4 | Return of the old Primary: it must not form a dual primary; it must follow the detect → rewind / basebackup / rejoin story, and it ends as a Replica pointing at the current leader (or a maintenance state). | holds |
| S5 | **DCS `/sync` is always a subset of PG `synchronous_standby_names`**: a name published into `/sync` must be in PG's current synchronous list, must have been confirmed caught up (`flush_lsn` has reached the Primary flush LSN recorded when the PG list changed, and is within `maximum_lag_on_syncnode`), and must actually be streaming in `pg_stat_replication`. The two writes have a symmetric order that keeps the subset true even when the cycle is interrupted: **add** — first expand the PG list and confirm catch-up, then expand `/sync`; **remove** — first shrink `/sync`, then shrink the PG list. When `synchronous_mode` is enabled, a Failover / Switchover candidate must belong to `/sync.sync_standby`; when `/sync` is missing, empty, or an unsatisfiable strict placeholder, no node may be promoted. There is a **transition window**: a newly promoted primary first publishes an empty `/sync`, or one that contains only itself, and standbys are filled in after they catch up. During that window `/sync` may shrink briefly or even become empty, and then no node can be promoted (failover is temporarily forbidden, but it is safe). `synchronous_mode_strict` affects only write blocking (it uses an unsatisfiable placeholder list, which is not `*`), and it does not take part in the election. Only Priority (`FIRST N`) mode is wired into the running path. | holds |

**Switchover with no candidate** (S1): `/failover` is `{"leader": "<旧主>", "candidate": null}`. After the old primary stops, it starts as a standby; its WAL is often the highest, and its timeline still equals `/timeline`, so the rewind-check marker does not exclude it from the election. Therefore the election does not hand the lock back to it by WAL: `failover.leader` does not acquire the lock (log `Not taking part in the election: this node is the leader switchover asked to step down`), and the other nodes skip it when they compare "who is healthiest". When a candidate is specified and that candidate can still take part, only the candidate acquires the lock, and nothing further is excluded. A manual failover does not write `leader`, and it excludes nobody. When there is no other eligible node, `POST /switchover` refuses before demotion (409, `No other running member can take over; refusing switchover without a candidate`), does not write `/failover`, and does not stop the database. `/failover` is cleared only after someone acquires the lock; when the key has already been written and there is no eligible node (for example the only replica stops after the request was accepted), the cluster is temporarily without a primary.

**`/failover` intent expires by itself (S3, SB-05)**: a non-empty switchover / failover intent carries the cluster `ttl` when it is written to `/failover` (the same TTL as the leader lease, reused rather than a new setting; `RaftDcs::set_failover_value` sets `ttl: Some(lock_ttl())` for a non-empty value, and the state machine expires it by the wall clock). Therefore "the key was written but nobody took over" is **bounded**: if the specified candidate does not acquire the lock within about `ttl` seconds (it is dead, it is never on the member list, or it disappears from the member list), after the key expires it is filtered out of the cluster view, the step-down logic immediately no longer sees the specified candidate, and the election returns to the normal "who is healthiest". This guarantees **election termination** — a dead candidate can no longer leave the cluster permanently without a primary (the liveness premise of S2). The clearing semantics are unchanged: writing an empty string after a successful lock acquisition still clears the key (an empty value carries no TTL). Scheduled switchover is not implemented; `/failover` carries only an immediate intent, and a short TTL does not cut off a future plan.

| ID | Statement | Status |
|----|-----------|--------|
| R1 | In the steady state, a Replica's replication upstream (`primary_conninfo`, or a cascading upstream) points at the current leader, or at a cascade source that the documentation explicitly allows. | holds |

**Exception (R2, D6)**: election eligibility compares the timeline the member published (the timeline `wal_position` is on; a lower bound when a replica cannot obtain an exact value, see 1a) and the local history evidence against the identity recorded in `/timeline`. **Note (R3, SB-07 Option A decided 2026-11-03)**: the member `conn_url` built by `touch_member` **contains only `host/port/dbname/user`, and never writes the superuser password**, so the password no longer enters the Raft log / `raft_log.wal` / `state_machine.json` / snapshot (these files no longer have a plaintext secret on disk). When they run the command they take the password from this node's own local `postgresql.superuser.password` and pass it in through the child process's `PGPASSWORD` environment variable (it never appears in argv, it is not written to disk, and logs are redacted by `redact_connstr`). Because the pg-ha deployment model is one superuser for the whole cluster, the local password is the peer's password. When `superuser.password` is empty, `PGPASSWORD` is not set, and the behavior is the same as a connection with no password.

## 6. Control-plane persistence and process recovery

| ID | Statement | Status |
|----|-----------|--------|
| P1 | After a process crashes and restarts, the Raft log and state machine restored from local disk must be able to rebuild a cluster view consistent with the **committed** semantics from before the crash (uncommitted log entries may be lost, in accordance with Raft). | holds |
| P2 | When the log-storage implementation changes from full JSON to an optimization such as an append-only WAL, it must keep P1 and the external HA semantics unchanged (M2). | holds |
| P3 | Failsafe **does not apply** under pg-ha's embedded RaftDcs architecture: a situation in which consensus cannot be reached is handled by lease-expiry fencing (the time bound of L5 / "lease-expiry fencing" in this file, which SB-04 calls R19); pg-ha explicitly does not support an external DCS, so the failsafe-related surface has been removed. | **decided (NO-GO, SB-04)** |
| P4 | If local persistence (including the WAL) fails, it must not, for a long time, present "memory/Raft has succeeded and the disk has not" with no degraded semantics. | holds |

**Degraded semantics of P4** (`crates/pg-ha-dcs/src/store.rs`):

- When a disk write fails, the storage trait methods (`save_vote`, `save_committed`, `append_to_log`, `delete_conflict_logs_since`, `purge_logs_upto`, `apply_to_state_machine`, `install_snapshot`) return `StorageError`, and they no longer return `Ok`. openraft therefore does not treat this write as persisted, and does not advance the commit or reply to the client.
- After openraft receives `StorageError`, it stops that node's Raft core (`Fatal`), and every Raft write afterwards fails. The process does not exit, and the log contains `ERROR Raft storage persistence failed (...)`.
- The consequence for HA: `get_cluster` reads the local state machine and returns a frozen old view, but every action that changes identity (acquiring the lock, renewing the lock) has to go through a Raft write, so this node can neither become leader nor remain leader. If it is currently holding the lock, the next renewal failure (`Ok(false)` or `Err`), or lease expiry (1a "leader lease"), is a demotion: write `standby.signal` and the rewind-check marker first, then stop the database; afterwards pg-ha starts it only as a standby (the 1a startup rules). After the leader key expires by TTL in the local view, it takes part in the election at most as a read-only standby. Acquiring the lock requires a Raft write and necessarily fails, so it does not appear again as a writable primary. The remaining majority of the cluster continues to serve.
- Recovery has to be done by hand: fix the disk, then restart pg-ha. The order of updating memory before disk is unchanged (WAL compaction needs the complete in-memory log), but a divergence is not reported as persisted.

**Related**: architecture Raft / DCS; `raft_log.wal`, `hard_state.json`, and so on.

**P3 decision record (SB-04, NO-GO)**: Patroni's failsafe is designed for an **external DCS** (etcd/consul) — when the DCS is down but the PostgreSQL cluster is still healthy, the primary confirms the topology with every member over REST and keeps serving, rather than demoting. What pg-ha uses is an **embedded Raft DCS colocated with the process**, so: (a) `RaftDcs::get_cluster` is a local read and never returns `Err`, and the failsafe entry `handle_dcs_error` is unreachable under RaftDcs; (b) the "cannot reach consensus" case is already handled, more conservatively and in a way that better matches the semantics of a consistency database, by lease-expiry fencing (see the time bound of L5 and the "lease-expiry fencing" subsection of this file, which SB-04 calls R19: a primary that fails to renew within `ttl - safety_margin` fences itself, and does not wait for DCS). So failsafe is both unreachable and redundant here. Because **an external DCS is explicitly not supported**, keeping it would only mislead, and its surface code has been removed. The `failsafe_mode` configuration field can still be parsed for backward compatibility, but it is a no-op (it no longer has any effect).

## 7. Pause, maintenance, and the watchdog

| ID | Statement | Status |
|----|-----------|--------|
| W1 | Under `pause` (or an equivalent maintenance mode): do not acquire the lock automatically / do not fail over automatically (consistent with the operations meaning of "pause"). | holds |
| W2 | The watchdog is fed only on the path where "this node considers itself a writable Primary"; when it loses the lock or is no longer Primary, it must stop feeding, to prevent a zombie primary. | holds |

**W2 implementation**: `Ha` holds a `Watchdog`.

- **Whether it can be primary**: in `required` mode, when the device does not exist or is not writable (the check does not open the device; opening it arms it; on non-Linux it is unavailable in every case), this node does not run for initialization and does not take part in the election (INFO/ERROR `Not taking part in the election: the watchdog is required but cannot be used on this node`), and it publishes `watchdog_failed: true` in the member record; other nodes do not yield to it, and `POST /switchover` and `POST /failover` reject it as a candidate (409, `Candidate '<c>' cannot use its required watchdog`). A D6 forced election (`pg-ha.accept_timeline_loss`) cannot bypass this rule.
- **Arming**: arm before `pg_ctl promote` (and also before writing the D6 pending), with a timeout of `ttl − safety_margin`; under `required`, if arming fails, do not promote, PostgreSQL remains a standby, and release the lock immediately. During Hold (for example pg-ha restarts while the primary is running), if it has not been armed yet, arm it; under `required`, a failure fences it as a lost lease.
- **Feeding and disarming** (`reconcile_watchdog`, called at the end of every cycle, after every lease fence, and at every step of shutdown; the only feed/disarm point): holding the lock and the lease is valid → feed (including while `stop_pending` is retrying the stop); PostgreSQL has stopped, or is observed in recovery → disarm; pause → disarm (W1); every remaining case (PostgreSQL may be writable and the lease is already invalid, for example the fencing stop failed) → **do not feed and do not disarm**, and if the stop does not finish within the timeout the kernel resets the machine. When this cycle's start or stop invalidates the observation, take one live observation (it has a timeout) before judging, and do not treat a PostgreSQL that has just been started as a standby as possibly writable; after a stop is confirmed successful, judge immediately and disarm before the start that follows. The device is opened with `O_CLOEXEC`: PostgreSQL and the other processes started by pg-ha do not inherit it; otherwise a child process holds the device, and the kernel timer does not stop after disarm either. Demotion, lease fencing, and the start of shutdown no longer disarm ahead of time.
- **Exit**: `Watchdog` has no `Drop` that disarms; when the HA engine panics, or the process exits without disarming, the device is closed without the magic character, and the kernel resets it when the timer expires. A graceful shutdown disarms and then exits only when PostgreSQL has already stopped (or it is a standby that does not hold the lock).
- The default is `mode: off`. `watchdog.safety_margin` is also used for the leader lease (L5). Known not to be closed: the timeout is counted from the moment of feeding and not from the renewal anchor; see section 9.

**Related**: operations pause and maintenance; architecture / code `watchdog.rs`.
